01The one idea

The format is defined by what it removes. Every element a video model reliably fails at has been deleted from the frame — and what's left is the one thing these models are genuinely good at.

Deleted from every clip

Objects held in hands · material transformations · camera movement · on-screen and garment text · dialogue · location changes inside a shot.

What's left

An adult woman, standing, in one real place, doing one small human movement. That's the entire content — and it lands in the narrow band where generated video reads as real.

Why this matters for us: our own recent batches failed on exactly the deleted list — held tools detaching, transformations fighting the subject for the frame, wide shots turning her into a silhouette, text rendering as noise, cuts appearing mid-clip. This genre wins by never attempting any of it.

The angle ladder — change between beats, never within one

All 131 clips use a locked static camera. Zero moves. That isn't laziness — a moving camera is where the model warps and swims. So we get "different angles" the free way: three locked setups, cut together.

Beat 1 · 0–5 s
Medium, eye level, straight on. Mid-thigh to crown. The format's proven frame — establishes her.
Beat 2 · 5–10 s
Medium-close, low 3/4, ~15° under. The angle change is the cut the viewer feels.
Beat 3 · 10–15 s
Wide, or a tight close. Pay off with either the world or the face. Never both.
Keep centre-x ≈ 0.50 in every beat. The angle changes; the centring never does.

05The four levers they leave on the table

① Persona lock — the moat

Competitors re-roll the woman constantly; ~a quarter of spliced clips change her mid-clip. That's why they get views and not followers. Lock one persona with a reference image and carry her across every beat and every reel. This is what converts a view-farm into a page.

② Light — the biggest untouched gap

100% of the sample is flat frontal midday daylight. That is the entire lighting design of a 1.3M-follower page. Golden-hour rim light, practicals, neon, night — all cost zero extra render time and instantly read as produced.

③ Elemental motion

The model fails at held objects and is good at ambient physics. The sample uses neither. Wind in hair and fabric, rain, steam, spray, dust in a light beam, falling petals — free differentiation, one per beat.

④ Cut to the music

Pick the track before assembling. At 120 bpm a beat is 0.5 s, so 5.000 s is exactly 10 beats and the cuts land clean. At 128 bpm use 5.625 s beats (3 bars). A cut on the downbeat is the whole difference between "edited" and "assembled".

06The hero concept — one motion, three worlds

She performs one single continuous motion. The world changes under her at every cut.

World 1
Rain-soaked neon street at night. Wet ground, magenta and cyan practicals.
World 2
Golden-hour rooftop. Low sun behind her, wind lifting her hair.
World 3
White cyc studio. Soft overhead, petals falling through frame.

It works because the prompt barely changes: subject block identical, framing block identical, motion block identical — only setting and light are swapped. That's exactly what the 2×5 s splice already does, pushed to its end. The cut lands on the same body position, so it reads as a teleport rather than an edit. Nothing in the 131 does this.

Two cheaper siblings: the match-cut wardrobe (identical pose and framing, only the outfit changes — turns the splice from artifact into deliberate effect), and the twin (the two-subject variant exists in ~10% of the set; used on purpose, the same persona twice in one frame is a real trick).

Heat is the ceiling, not VRAM. Hold the card ≤40 °C for 60 s before every render — it pins at 93–95 °C and throttles 2,835→1,612 MHz. Peak VRAM is only ~46 of 96 GB. Launch --highvram --vram-headroom 2. Never --use-sage-attention.
  1. Persona pass — generate 3–5 reference portraits, keep the ones that hold across angles.
  2. Two-clip gate — build ONE full 15 s reel, run qa.py --beats 3 --duration 15, look at it. Only then scale. Bad batches are otherwise discovered at clip 20.
  3. Ten-reel batch — one persona, ten world/wardrobe/angle triplets.
  4. Post and measure before batching 80. Retention at 5 s and 10 s tells you whether beats 2 and 3 earn their render time. It's the only metric that matters.

08The two gates that stop a batch

H3 licence §V.4 restricts distribution of H3 outputs in the EU, UK, South Korea and the US — and a public Facebook page publishes to all four. Resolve before the first post. The standing answer is design on H3, render the shipping frames on Wan 2.2.
Adults only, plain opaque fabric, platform-SFW. Sheer fabric rendering underlying anatomy is what gets pages swept — and it's unnecessary: the strongest measured performers are plain cotton. Use the platform's own AI label; it's free, it doesn't suppress reach, and EU AI Act Art. 50 has applied since 2026-08-02.

09How the numbers were kept honest

Three detectors were built. One was rebuilt and two were thrown away — the failures are more instructive than the successes.

✅ Splice detector — rebuilt

ffmpeg's scene score reported 0 cuts on clips that are visibly two halves, because a join between two similar clips scores below threshold. One clip is a brunette in one kitchen then a blonde in another — and scored zero. Replacement uses median+MAD outliers on frame differences: it scores that clip z=25.4 at 0.502. Positive control: a known splice at 4.0 s of 8.0 s → detected at exactly 4.0.

✗ Multi-subject by body shape — discarded

Flagged 53 of 69, including clips already confirmed by eye as one woman. An arm held away from the torso makes a gap that reads as a second body.

✗ Multi-subject by head blobs — discarded

Scored 7/12 against hand-checked controls, every error a false positive. So subject count is reported from watching, not from a detector: 2 of 32 watched had two women (~10%).

Also corrected against the full set: an early "subject lit ~20 luma levels brighter than the surround" came from a 51-clip subset and did not survive n=131 — the real figure is ~8 levels, a mild bias rather than a vignette. It is not a lighting rule.

10The AI tells to check for

Garbled garment text — the loudest

Every graphic tee in the set is nonsense on inspection: "HAJIE U", "Panye OKis", "RAHKENIBLE", "Rander Mesion", "BacIVORLIN", "RAIUCEN", "RENCE WALK". This one tell is why plain fabric is mandatory.
